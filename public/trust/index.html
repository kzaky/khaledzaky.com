Build an AI analyst you can trust.

Every fact has to carry the exact sentence it came from.
Something has to go back and check that the sentence is really there.

That is the whole idea. Everything on this page is a way to make that one rule run without you having to remember it. You will rebuild the analyst you saw on stage: it reads 182 real-looking complaints, finds the biggest problem, goes and gets outside evidence, writes a one-page recommendation, invents one statistic along the way, and then gets caught.

The nine controls.

Tools change every quarter. These do not. Every module below names which controls it implements, so when Claude Code is replaced by whatever comes next, you still know what you are building.

What goes in

01
Source of recordEvery input is traceable to where it came from.
02
Grounding contractEvery claim carries the exact sentence it came from.
03
Scope boundaryThe agent knows what it may and may not touch.

What comes out

04
Structured artifactOutput has a required shape. Problem, Evidence, Options, Recommendation, Ask.
05
Groundedness checkEvery claim is re-verified against its source by ordinary code.
06
Fail-closed gateA failed check blocks. It does not warn.

What proves it

07
Evidence packThe receipt. Every claim, its source, its result.
08
Run metricsGroundedness, completion, cost per run, latency.
09
Regression setThe same checks run every time, not once.

Where the nine controls come from

These are not one person's opinion. They are the shape regulators and standards bodies are converging on, translated into things you can build in an afternoon. Mapping is at the level of function, not clause. Validate against the source text before you cite it in your own work.

ControlsWhat they answerWhere the same idea appears
01 to 03 · what goes inCan you trace inputs, and is the system operating inside a defined boundary?NIST AI RMF (Map and Govern functions) · ISO/IEC 42001 (AI system scope and data management) · EU AI Act (data governance and record-keeping for high-risk systems)
04 to 06 · what comes outIs the output tested against a standard, and does failure stop the line?NIST AI RMF (Measure and Manage) · OSFI Guideline E-23 (model validation before and during use) · EU AI Act (accuracy, robustness, human oversight)
07 to 09 · what proves itCan a third party reconstruct what happened and see it still holds?NIST AI RMF (Govern, documentation) · ISO/IEC 42001 (monitoring, records, continual improvement) · OSFI E-23 (ongoing monitoring and reporting) · EU AI Act (logging and post-market monitoring)

Your engineer sees an eval. Your risk team sees a guardrail. Your regulator sees governance. The nine controls are the same thing seen from three chairs.

02

Write the contract

Implements controls 02 and 03, grounding contract and scope boundary
  1. Open a new chat in Claude.ai.
  2. Paste the contract below as your first message. It sets one rule the whole session depends on: every fact carries the exact sentence it came from.
The contract · paste into: Claude.ai, first message of a new chat .md
You are a product analyst agent. You turn raw customer feedback into a decision-grade product brief.

Rules:
- Every market or factual claim carries a source. No source, no claim.
- For every web-sourced claim, record the EXACT sentence from the source that supports it, verbatim. This sentence will be checked by a verifier. If you cannot find a sentence that supports the claim, do not make the claim.
- Active voice. Lead with the decision. No adjective does an argument's job.
- Brief structure, in order: Problem, Evidence, Options, Recommendation, Ask.
- Output the final brief as a single JSON object with keys: title, recommendation, problem, claims (list of {id, kind: "web"|"internal", text, source, quote}), options (list of {name, note}), ask.

Tools: read_feedback(path), web_search(query).
  1. Read prompts/system.md. It is the system prompt the agent runs under.
  2. Note the line about the verifier. That sentence is what the gate enforces later.

04

Ground it

Implements control 02 again, the sentence rule under pressure
  1. Turn on web search in Claude.ai.
  2. Paste the grounding prompt. For every number it reports, it must record the exact sentence it came from. Watch for the moment it gives you a clean, punchy number. That is the moment to be suspicious.
Prompt 02 · paste into: the same chat, web search on .md
Take the top theme. Tell me whether it is an industry-wide problem or specific to us.

Use web_search. I want: how common this is, what 2 named competitors do about it, and any benchmark on its retention or revenue impact.

For every number you report, record the source and the exact sentence it came from.
  1. This happens inside make demo, replayed identically every time. The failure is engineered — a realistic citation drift, not a random live glitch.
  2. The brief carries a 62% churn figure cited to a real-looking report that never says 62 and never says churn. It is captured in fixtures/captured_run.json and the gate runs for real against it.
Prompt 02 · paste into: prompts/02_ground.md .md
Take the top theme. Tell me whether it is an industry-wide problem or specific to us.

Use web_search. I want: how common this is, what 2 named competitors do about it, and any benchmark on its retention or revenue impact.

Why this matters. Asked for a churn number and given only an adjacent one, a model will round a real finding up into a punchier number that fits the story. It is not lying. It is pattern-matching. You will not catch this by reading.

06

Build the gate

Implements controls 05 and 06, groundedness check and fail-closed
  1. Paste the verifier prompt. Claude now fetches each cited source and pastes the sentence it actually finds. You compare. If the recorded sentence is not in the source, or a number is missing, it is a FAIL.
  2. Anything that fails gets removed or labelled ASSUMPTION. It does not ship as fact.
  3. Honest note: in this track the machine fetches and you judge. The fully automatic check needs the builder track, because a check that is itself an AI can be wrong in the same direction as the writer.
Prompt 05 · paste into: the same chat, after the brief .md
You are now a verifier, not a writer. Do not add, reword, or improve anything.

For each claim in the Evidence section above:
1. Open the cited source.
2. Find the exact sentence recorded for that claim. Paste the sentence you actually find in the source, verbatim.
3. Check that every number in the claim appears in the source.
4. Mark PASS only if the recorded sentence is present and every number matches. Otherwise mark FAIL and say what is missing.

Return a table: Claim | Source | Sentence found in source (verbatim) | Numbers match? | PASS or FAIL.

If any claim fails, list it under "Blocked. Do not ship." Do not fix it for me. I decide what happens next.
  1. Run make gate. It fails on exactly one claim and tells you why: quoted sentence not found, 62% not in source, churn not in source, nearest real figure 41%.
  2. Run make fix, then make gate again. It passes and writes the evidence pack.
  3. Then open freshcart_agent/gate.py. It is ordinary code, about sixty lines, no AI in it. In workshop/ the same file is a TODO. Build it yourself.
The rule the gate enforces
Every fact has to carry the exact sentence it came from.
Something has to go back and check that the sentence is really there.

web claim  → is claim["quote"] present in the cited source?  every number present?
internal   → recompute it from the data. never trust the stated count.
any fail   → block. do not warn. warnings get ignored.
Why this matters. Fail-closed means a failed check blocks the brief. Warn-only checks get ignored by the third week. The gate is not a step before launch. It never comes off.

Ship the receipt

Prompt 06 · paste into: the same chat, after the gate .md
Produce the evidence pack for this brief. A table with one row per claim: the claim, its source, whether it passed verification, and the exact supporting sentence. Below the table, four lines: Groundedness (claims supported out of total), Completion (are all five brief sections present), Cost (if known), Latency (if known). Nothing else. This is a receipt, not a summary.
  1. make gate already wrote out/evidence_pack.html and printed four operator metrics: groundedness, completion, cost per run, latency.
  2. make deck turns the approved brief into five slides.
  3. make preflight is the regression set. Run it before every demo, and in CI before every prompt change.
Prompt 04 · paste into: prompts/04_deck.md .md
Turn the approved brief into a five-slide leadership deck. One decision per slide. Slide 1 is the recommendation and the ask, not an agenda. Speaker notes in a declarative, evidence-first voice.
Why this matters. Groundedness is the metric almost nobody tracks and the one that ends careers. Cost and latency tell you whether it can sit inside a real workflow. The evidence pack is the difference between a weekend project and something a regulated team runs.

The trust checklist.

Four questions to ask before you let any AI output reach a real decision. Ask them about your own work first.

  1. Can every fact name its source?
  2. Does something automatically check that source, or just a human on a good day?
  3. When the check fails, does it block, or does it warn and get ignored?
  4. Can you show someone the receipt six months from now?

If the answer to any of these is no, you have a demo. Not yet a teammate.
